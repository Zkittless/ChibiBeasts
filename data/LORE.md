# 🐾 The ChibiBeasts Lore Bible
### *The Sundering of the Loom*

---

## I. The Creation Myth

Before there were beasts, before there were trainers, before there was even a *world* to stand on — there was only **the Loom**.

The Loom was not a place. It was the act of weaving itself: an endless, formless process spinning raw possibility into shape. It had no name, because there was no one to name it, and no mouth to speak one.

Then, in a single instant that some call the **First Stitch**, the Loom wove four threads tighter than any others — and they woke up.

These four became the **First Voices** — Prism, Loom, Aspect and Pillar: not gods exactly, not quite alive in the way a fairy or a goblin is alive, but *aware*. Each First Voice grasped one piece of what the Loom was doing and gave it a shape a small creature could hold.

- **Prism**, the Architect of Form, who believed reality needed structure — edges, geometry, order. From a shard of its own crystalline heart, it shaped **Prismite**, a small porcelain kitten with diamond eyes, and sent it out to see whether *order* could be gentle.
- **Loom**, the First Voice of Time, who believed reality needed memory — a thread connecting what-was to what-will-be. It pulled loose a strand of itself, crimson and ticking, and shaped it into a hamster with a tiny clock-gear shell: **Twine**, sent to learn whether *time* could be kind.
- **Aspect**, the Architect of Change, who believed reality needed to be soft enough to become anything. It let a piece of its own shifting, galaxy-colored self bubble free into **Gloop**, a slime full of swirling starlight, sent to discover whether *change* could be safe.
- **Pillar**, the Architect of Foundation, who believed reality needed something that would not break. It cracked off a fragment of bark and ancient moss and stone and shaped **Barkley**, a small dragon hatchling wearing its own eggshell like a helmet, sent to prove that *steadiness* could still be playful.

Each First Voice sent its small creation down into the unfinished world — alone, unfinished too, but capable of choosing a companion. That is the oldest story ChibiBeasts tells about itself: **the world began with four lonely ideas, and they did not want to be lonely anymore.**

This is why, to this day, every trainer's journey begins with a choice between **Prismite**, **Twine**, **Gloop**, or **Barkley**. You are not picking a pet. You are continuing a four-sided conversation that started before the world had a floor to stand on.

---

## II. The Five Divine Collections

The First Voices didn't stop at four companions. As the Loom kept weaving, larger ideas crystallized into Divine beings — vast, beautiful, and a little bit beyond understanding. They organized themselves (or were organized, nobody's quite sure) into five Collections, each one a different answer to the question *"what does it mean for something to be eternal?"*

| Collection | Domain | Members |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmic Creators** | The raw stuff reality is made of | Singularity, Astraea, Chronos, Aetherius |
| **Architects of Reality** | Beginnings, endings, and the space between | Genesis, Terminus, Paradox |
| **Celestial Loom** | Fate, consequence, and the threads connecting all things | Horizon, Karma, Zodiac |
| **Primordial Aspects** | Forces too big for a single shape — gravity, decay, combustion | Abyss, Nebula, Supernova |
| **Mythological Pillars** | The things that hold the world up and refuse to fall | Nirvana, Asgard, Atlas |

Trainers don't typically *meet* Divines so much as **earn the right to be noticed by one** — through raids, through deep exploration of the Celestial Loom biome, or (the long way) through the loyalty of an ordinary beast raised with enough care that something Divine takes interest.

The five Collections are not rivals. They're closer to five departments of the same impossible organization, each one quietly making sure the world keeps being a world. Most of the time, anyway.

## III. The Sundering — What Actually Happened

The First Voices and the **Architects of Reality** are different groups. The Voices are the four ideas carried by the starters. Genesis, Terminus and Paradox are the three named Divine companions of the Architects of Reality collection. Neither group is a hidden villain.

The Fraying began when an old protective stitch was left running after the danger it was made to contain had passed. Its instruction was to keep unfinished possibilities safe. Without a way to recognize growth, it kept pulling new threads back into an old pattern. The hollow southwest of Sable's forge is where that loop became visible.

The pressure has different consequences. **Altered Divines** are changed, stable companions. **Corrupted beasts** are caught in an overwhelming protection loop and need help loosening it. These are separate states; the three Altered Divine companions are not the guild raid bosses. **Ancient** encounters are meetings with the older forms of Chronos, Genesis and Abyss.

A successful raid leaves the creature alive. Trainers battle, guard, tend one another and respond to its pattern until it can settle. Materials are shed or offered, rather than taken as trophies. Every active contributor receives rewards, and repeated successful rescues build toward a companion choosing to join them.

The campaign's answer is to loosen the old stitch and give the hollow room to grow. The World Tree Seed brings a living memory of how to begin again; it is hatched and brought back as a companion. A player's choice of care, curiosity or courage shapes the scene and title, and each choice restores the hollow.

## IV. Why You're Doing Any of This

Putting it plainly, for the player: **you are not an exterminator, a soldier, or a chosen one.** You're a trainer in a world that is, very literally, still being woven — and every beast you catch, raise, battle with, and bond with is part of how that weaving gets *gentler* instead of rougher.

- Catching and hatching beasts mirrors what the First Voices did at the very beginning: giving a small, unfinished idea a companion so it isn't alone.
- Battling isn't conquest — beasts in ChibiBeasts don't fight to the death, they fight to test and grow, the same way Prismite, Twine, Gloop, and Barkley were sent out to "see whether their Architect's idea could survive contact with the world."
- Raiding a Corrupted or Ancient beast makes room for an overwhelming idea to settle through teamwork and care. The creature survives the encounter.
- The Bestiary exists because the Loom *itself* seems to want to be witnessed — every first catch in a server is treated, narratively, as a tiny stitch finishing correctly.

This is also why the tone stays warm even at endgame: there's no final boss to slay and no apocalypse to prevent. The "stakes" are always really just *"help this unfinished thing settle into a shape it can be happy with."* That's true of a wild Slime in the Whispering Woods and it's true of a server-shaking raid encounter. Same instinct, different scale.

## V. Naming & Tone Guide (for future content)

To keep new beasts, items, and systems feeling like they belong in this world:

- **Beasts** are never "monsters" in dialogue/flavor text — they're referred to the way you'd refer to a animal companion: by what they *do*, not what they *are for*. ("A delicate woodland sprite that flutters through enchanted forests," not "a low-tier nature-type unit.")
- **Divine beasts' descriptions always gesture at a cosmic-scale concept made small and almost-graspable** — they're vast ideas, chibi-sized. Keep the contrast between "incomprehensible cosmic force" and "fits in your party" — that gap *is* the charm of the whole game.
- **Items and materials use a kitchen-table-magic register**: things smell like strawberries, taste like cinnamon, look like candy — even Legendary/Divine-tier items stay tactile and a little silly, never grimdark. A "Tear of the Creator" should still sound like something you could (carefully) put in your pocket.
- **No real death.** Beasts faint, eggs fail to hatch and need to be tried again, raid creatures settle or step back, and never die. This isn't a content restriction so much as a tone commitment — it's baked into why raids work the way they do above.
- **The Loom is never a villain**, and there's deliberately no single antagonist. Instability and Corrupted protection loops is closer to weather than to evil — something to respond to with care and teamwork, not something to hate.

## VI. Payoffs in the Playable Campaign

- Chapter five brings the first four relics together and awards the **World Tree Seed**. The Seed incubates for 24 hours. It must hatch before the final chapter is complete.
- Chapter seven finishes **Sable's clasp** and delivers it to Maren. It is a repaired fastening for her old coat, an act of attention with a recipient and a purpose.
- Chapter eight reunites **Maren and Orren**. Their silence is addressed in the scene, rather than kept as an unresolved tease.
- Chapter nine identifies the Fraying as a protective loop that has outlived its purpose. The Corrupted encounters teach the same response the finale asks of the player.
- Chapter ten asks for an actual answer: **care, curiosity or courage**. The player and their hatched companion loosen the stitch, the hollow opens, and a new root grows. The journal stays complete and all five NPCs have post-story dialogue.

## VII. The Four Bond Awakenings

The starter's shape grows from shared experience. At beast level 15, bond 30 and happiness 70, `/evolve` awakens it without a catalyst. The companion keeps its nickname, training, disposition, earned stat boosts, Ambrosia bonus, gear and party assignments.

- **Prismite → Prismara:** the small crystalline kitten becomes a long-eared crystal serval with a shard mantle and three faceted tails. Its order has become shelter: it begins battles with a protective shield.
- **Twine → Timestitch:** the round clockwork hamster becomes a spring-legged jerboa with a spool back and ribbon tail. It carries memory into motion, with an additional action every third turn.
- **Gloop → Nebuloom:** the round galaxy slime becomes a four-flipper jelly salamander with three golden star fins and a crescent tail. Its healing skill is stronger; change makes more room for care.
- **Barkley → Rootwarden:** the hatchling becomes a broad forest drake with leaf wings, wooden antlers and root paws. The eggshell helmet becomes a chestplate. Guarding restores health, and its body absorbs part of incoming damage.

The awakenings are Divine starter-family forms, outside the five Divine Collections. They are earned by bonding, never random wild encounters or egg outcomes.
